# 2026-11-07 research: CreditDecoding + CoDR + RDD + FlashProve -> CWTC

## Findings

### CreditDecoding — Trace Credit (arXiv:2510.06133v3, May 2026)
Parallel dLLM decoding confirms only high-confidence positions each step.
Key inefficiency: models often predict the correct target token several
steps before its confidence becomes high enough to decode; the gap forces
repeated remasking of already-correct tokens. Trace Credit accumulates
historical logits across steps as a token-level prior, fused with current
logits to boost correct-but-underconfident tokens. Training-free; up to
5.48x speedup, +0.48 accuracy on LLaDA-8B-Instruct.
Diagnosis absorbed: a token can be right long before it is confident
enough to commit; re-proving it every step is wasted work. Mechanism NOT
ported: raw logit fusion — it changes the model distribution and carries
no guarantee about what gets boosted. CWTC keeps the diagnosis and
replaces the mechanism (see below).

### CoDR — Confidence-Drift Remasking (arXiv:2610.08833, Oct 2026)
A committed token's confidence can drop from its sparse commit-time
context to the denser context available later ("confidence drift").
CoDR estimates drift for all committed positions in only k forward
passes via k-partition probing, then remasks and regenerates only the
tokens the model no longer endorses. Training-free, sampler-agnostic.
Diagnosis absorbed: endorsement is temporal — a commit-time snapshot is
not the whole story. Mechanism NOT ported: CoDR's response is remasking
after the fact; CWTC's ledger records endorsement *before* the commit,
so drift is priced into the commit decision itself. Also close in
spirit to XIIV's own CDR (Nov 2, certificate-drift remasking) — CoDR
tracks confidence drift, CDR tracks certificate drift; neither keeps a
per-token endorsement ledger.

### RDD — Reversible Diffusion Decoding (arXiv:2602.00150, Feb 2026)
Irreversible block commitments can stagnate the reverse process; RDD
detects stagnation as a state-dependent failure and backtracks to
earlier blocks without recomputation via cached states, with
confidence-guided re-masking of uncertain tokens. Training-free.
Diagnosis absorbed: a stalled canvas is a failure mode in its own
right, not just slow progress. Mechanism NOT ported: RDD's rollback is
to cached model states; CWTC does not need it — the credit ledger
itself is the anti-stall device (a position whose leader keeps earning
credit always makes progress toward commit). Stagnation-triggered
rollback to the last certificate-clean snapshot stays on the shelf
for a later sampler.

### FlashProve (Anthropic, open-sourced 2026-11-04, Apache 2.0)
Zero-knowledge proof that an answer was generated by a committed set
of weights: proves *origin*, not correctness. 70-95x inference cost,
1,024-token context, greedy decoding only, ~18.78 MB proof, CPU
verification in ~17 s. Watch-tier only: XIIV's certificates prove the
*content* satisfies a checkable property; FlashProve proves *who* said
it. Different axis, no adoption path — but the contrast sharpens
XIIV's claim: a certificate you can verify from the token list alone,
with no 70x overhead and no weights in the loop.

### Also noted (watch tier, not adopted)
- Re-evaluating Confidence Remasking (arXiv:2606.12232): remasking gains
  are setting-dependent; under greedy decoding WINO/Saber/COVER perform
  on par with Fast-dLLM at short block lengths. Honesty note for all
  remasking-family samplers: the dLLM itself, not the remasking rule,
  may be the bottleneck. CWTC's falsifiers are therefore stub-based
  mechanism checks, not capability claims.
- LCR vs TPP (arXiv:2609.34509): low-confidence remasking exponentially
  suppresses lower-probability tokens; top-probability position
  selection restores diversity. Watch for the serving rig; CWTC is
  greedy and deterministic, so the diversity axis does not apply.
- Mündler et al., CFG-constrained decoding for dLLMs (ICLR 2026):
  additive infilling reduced to CFG ∩ regular-language emptiness.
  Same LAVE family already absorbed; XIIV's verifier stays the
  lighter per-commit gate, not a grammar-intersection check.

## The pick: CWTC — Certificate-Weighted Trace Credit

Trace Credit's diagnosis (right-before-confident) + XIIV's
guarantee-native thesis, with a mechanism that is neither a port of
CreditDecoding nor a variant of CDR/CCCA:

- A per-position **credit ledger**. At each step, the leading
  certificate-safe candidate at a masked position earns credit equal
  to its current probability — but ONLY if it passes the independent
  no-repeat gate against the current canvas. A candidate that fails
  the gate earns exactly zero, however confident. No logits are ever
  modified or fused: the model distribution is untouched, so the
  guarantee stays clean — every committed token was certificate-safe
  at commit time AND repeatedly certificate-safe before it.
- A position commits on either of two keys: the confidence path
  (top-1 safe probability >= threshold, as usual) or the credit path
  (accumulated credit >= threshold while the token is the current
  leading safe candidate). A credit-driven commit is therefore a token
  the model kept endorsing, safely, across steps — temporal
  endorsement, not a single-pass spike.
- Credit **decays** (multiplicative, per step) and is per
  (position, token): a token endorsed once then abandoned can never
  commit later on stale credit. The ledger is the audit trail: the
  cwtc-v1 certificate itemizes every commit with its credit at commit,
  steps endorsed, and whether it was credit-driven or confidence-driven.
- Fail-closed like the rest of the family: stall budget + max-steps
  cap; an incomplete run reports completed=False with masks remaining.

Why this is the most promising of today's findings: it is the only one
that attacks a *different axis* from the last six samplers (temporal
endorsement / wasted re-proving, not commit gating, repair, or
consensus), it is implementable with zero GPU and zero training, and
its falsifiers are crisp on scripted stubs (a stubborn low-confidence
stub completes under CWTC and cannot complete under the confidence-only
policy in the same module).
