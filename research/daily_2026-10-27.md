## Part A — research findings (2024–2026, masked diffusion LMs)
1. **EB-Sampler** (Ben-Hamu et al., arXiv:2505.24857, May 2025):
   entropy-bounded unmasking, unmasks several tokens per forward pass
   within an error tolerance; claimed ~2–3× speedup at no quality loss
   on coding/math. Fit: CLOSE to XIIV's existing CGAU (certificate-gated
   adaptive unmasking) — a speed lever, not a new thesis.
2. **DEMASK** (Ringel et al., arXiv:2604.02560, Apr 2026):
   dependency-guided parallel decoding; bounds total-variation distance
   of the parallel sample under sub-additivity; 1.7–2.2× on Dream-7B.
   Fit: same family as (1); a dependency predictor is a new module,
   not a free win on XIIV's CPU box.
3. **Info-Gain Sampler** (Yang et al., arXiv:2602.18176, 2026):
   chooses unmasking by downstream uncertainty reduction, not just
   local confidence; reasoning accuracy +3.6% claimed. Fit: principled,
   but its gain is measured on frontier-scale models, not a 1B CPU run.
4. **UPO** (Hong et al., ICLR 2026): learned unmasking policy as a
   KL-regularized MDP, beating max-confidence on Sudoku by 11.2pp.
   Fit: needs policy training — not a no-GPU drop-in.
5. **Self-speculative decoding for dLLMs** (Gao et al.,
   arXiv:2510.04147, Oct 2025): the dLLM drafts and verifies itself,
   up to 3.46× with identical output to stepwise decoding. Fit: the
   strongest speed idea here, but "identical output" is measured on
   LLaDA/Dream, not XIIV, and XIIV's verifier is certificate-based,
   not logits-based — a port, not an invention.

## Part A — candidate for a later falsifier probe (NOT claimed, NOT measured)
**Certificate-entropy joint unmasking:** unmask a position only if it
is BOTH below an entropy bound (EB-Sampler's speed lever) AND
certificate-compatible (CGAU's gate). The falsifier is the same shape as
CGAU's: if joint gating is slower or less valid than CGAU alone on a
toy checkable task, kill it. This is a candidate, not an XIIV invention
yet — it combines two published mechanisms and earns the name only by
passing its own probe.
