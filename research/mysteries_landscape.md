# The Mysteries Landscape: Undeciphered Scripts & Historical Unknowns as Verified-Solution Targets

**Purpose.** Map the world's famous unsolved historical mysteries as candidate targets for a guarantee-native AI system (XIIV), under Fahad's capability axis: a mystery counts as "solved at 97–99% confidence" only when that confidence is *measured* — the fraction of surviving evidence consistently explained/decoded under one system, validated on held-out material the system was not fitted on. Self-reported confidence, press-release confidence, and "the model sounds sure" do not count.

**Claim labels used throughout:** `[peer-reviewed]` published in a refereed venue · `[single-study]` one group's result, not independently replicated · `[contested]` active expert dispute · `[popular claim]` media/amateur claim without expert acceptance.

**The two classes:**
- **Pattern-limited (a):** the evidence survives and contains the answer; the pattern has not been found. A correct solution is *verifiable* — it can be checked against held-out evidence. These are legitimate targets.
- **Evidence-limited (b):** the decisive evidence was destroyed, never recorded, or never existed in recoverable form. No system — human, AI, or ASI — can honestly exceed a hard confidence ceiling, because multiple incompatible answers fit all surviving evidence equally well. The ceiling is set by information theory (Shannon's *unicity distance*: below a certain amount of text, many keys fit), not by intelligence.

## 1. The undeciphered scripts

### 1.1 Voynich Manuscript (Beinecke MS 408), Italy, c. 1404–1438
- **Corpus:** ~240 pages, ~35,000–38,000 words, ~170,000+ characters, ~20–30 distinct glyphs. Radiocarbon-dated vellum (1404–1438) `[peer-reviewed]`. Six illustrated sections (herbal, astronomical, biological, pharmaceutical, recipes). Two statistical "dialects" (Currier A and B) and five scribal hands identified (Davis, 2020) `[peer-reviewed]`.
- **State of the art:** A century of failed attacks by professional cryptanalysts (WWI/WWII codebreakers included). Statistical work (Montemurro & Zanette, 2013) finds language-like keyword structure `[peer-reviewed]`. Hauer & Kondrak (2016/2018, Univ. of Alberta) ran language identification against 380 languages and got Hebrew as top match, then produced a first-line "decoding" via anagramming — see §4 for why experts rejected the framing. Cheshire's 2019 "proto-Romance" claim collapsed within days (§4). Timm & Schinner's self-citation/autocopying model shows the text *could* have been generated by a medieval hoaxer following simple rules, which no test has yet excluded `[peer-reviewed]`.
- **Why unsolved:** Three unknowns are stacked: (1) is it a cipher, an unknown language in an invented script, a lost shorthand — or generated pseudo-text with no plaintext at all? (2) No bilingual text exists. (3) No proposed source language has produced a reading that survives independent application to new pages.
- **Class:** **Pattern-limited, conditionally.** The corpus is *large* — far above any plausible unicity distance. If a real language+cipher underlies it, a correct key would read all ~35,000 words consistently and would verify itself on held-out pages. The honest blocker is search-space and system identification, not evidence volume. If it is generated pseudo-text, the "solution" would instead be a verified reconstruction of the generation process — a different, also checkable claim.

### 1.2 Indus Valley Script, c. 2600–1900 BCE
- **Corpus:** ~3,700–5,000 inscriptions (counts vary by catalogue), **average length ≈ 5 signs**, longest known text 26 signs; sign inventory ~400–700 depending on how variants are split (Mahadevan's list: 417). Total sign tokens ≈ under 25,000, but almost all in name-tag-length strings `[peer-reviewed]`.
- **State of the art:** A century of attempts; ~100 published "decipherments," none accepted. Parpola's Dravidian rebus readings (fish = *mīn*, "fish/star") cover a handful of signs, unproven `[contested]`. Rao et al. (Science, 2009) showed the sign sequences' conditional entropy sits in the range of natural languages `[peer-reviewed]`; Farmer, Sproat & Witzel replied that the comparison sets were unrepresentative and that the brevity and total absence of long texts suggest the signs may not encode speech at all `[peer-reviewed, contested both ways]`. In January 2025 the Tamil Nadu government offered a **US$1 million prize** for a decipherment accepted by the scholarly community — unclaimed as of latest reporting `[popular/official]`.
- **Why unsolved:** No bilingual text; underlying language unidentified (Dravidian? Austroasiatic? isolate?); no descendant script tradition (a ~1,500-year gap to Brahmi); and the texts are probably mostly names/titles — the semantic content needed to *check* a reading barely exists.
- **Class:** **Evidence-limited in practice, pattern-limited in principle.** A pattern (if the signs encode language) presumably exists, but verification is the wall: a system can fit 5-sign strings with many incompatible keys, and there is no held-out long text and no known language to validate against. Absent new archaeology (a bilingual, or substantially longer texts), no honest confidence above ~50–60% for any full decipherment is defensible — and even that requires the linguistic-status question (is it writing?) to resolve first.

### 1.3 Linear A (Minoan Crete), c. 1800–1450 BCE
- **Corpus:** ~1,370–1,530 documents, only **~7,400 sign tokens total** — about three to four A4 pages of text; ~90% administrative (tablets, roundels, nodules); ~97 sign types `[peer-reviewed: Computational Linguistics systematic review, 2024]`.
- **State of the art:** Uniquely, Linear A is *half*-read: because its descendant Linear B was deciphered, most syllabic sign values can be borrowed, so texts can be transliterated and "pronounced" — but they yield no known language. The numeral system and the fraction values are genuinely solved (Corazza et al., J. Archaeological Science, 2020) `[peer-reviewed]`. Computational reviews conclude corpus size is the binding constraint — one cited estimate holds that ~10,000–12,000 text samples would be needed for statistical decipherment approaches.
- **Why unsolved:** The underlying "Minoan" language appears to be an isolate (or at least unrelated to any well-attested family); no bilingual; corpus ~7.4k tokens, much of it lists of names + commodities + numerals, with very little continuous grammar-bearing text.
- **Class:** **Pattern-limited but corpus-starved.** Verification *would* be possible — a correct language identification would suddenly make hundreds of transliterated words resolve into a consistent lexicon and grammar, checkable across tablets. The evidence that survives is probably sufficient to *confirm* a correct guess but not to *find* one by search. Honest ceiling today: sound values ~high confidence; semantic decipherment low until the language is identified by independent means.

### 1.4 Rongorongo (Rapa Nui / Easter Island)
- **Corpus:** ~24–27 authentic wooden objects (tablets, a staff, ornaments), **~14,000–15,000 glyph tokens**, ~400–600 glyph types in Barthel's catalogue (many likely allographs/compounds). Written in reverse boustrophedon. A 2024 radiocarbon study dated one tablet's wood to 1493–1509, supporting pre-European-contact invention `[peer-reviewed: Scientific Reports]`.
- **State of the art:** No glyph can be read with confidence. Partial structural identifications exist — a possible lunar calendar sequence on the Mamari tablet (Barthel), genealogical patterns (Butinov) — none verified `[single-study/contested]`. Fischer's 1990s "decipherment" (a procreation chant reading) was rejected by specialists. The literacy tradition was destroyed in the 1860s (slave raids, epidemics, missionary suppression) before any bilingual record was made; 19th-century informant testimony (e.g., Metoro's readings for Bishop Jaussen) is inconsistent and generally judged unreliable.
- **Why unsolved:** The spoken language (Rapa Nui) *is* known — unusually favourable — but the glyph-to-sound mapping has no anchor: no bilingual, no confirmed word matches, uncertain sign inventory (which glyphs are variants?), and an open question whether the system is full phonetic writing or a partly mnemonic/proto-writing system.
- **Class:** **Pattern-limited, medium corpus.** ~14k tokens is workable *if* the system is phonetic and the readings are chant-formulaic (repetition would help). Verification path exists in principle: a correct key must read multiple independent tablets into consistent Old Rapa Nui consistent with the known language. Honest ceiling: potentially high *if* a key is found and cross-validates; today, no proposed reading exceeds low confidence because none has cross-validated.

### 1.5 Phaistos Disc (Crete, c. 1850–1450 BCE)
- **Corpus:** **One object.** 241–242 stamped sign tokens, 45 distinct sign types, 61 divider-separated groups, spiral layout, both sides of a single clay disc. One possible matching sign on a bronze axe (Arkalochori) and a sealing are the only claimed parallels `[peer-reviewed]`.
- **State of the art:** Dozens of published "decipherments" (as Greek, Luwian, a hymn, a calendar, a board game…) — mutually incompatible, none accepted. Philologist John Chadwick's verdict remains the field's: even if the true reading were revealed, *"it would be quite impossible to convince anyone else that his was the one and only possible solution."*
- **Why unsolved:** A single short text in an unknown language with no parallel corpus sits far below the unicity distance: the same 242 signs can be fitted by indefinitely many internally-consistent readings, with zero held-out material to discriminate between them.
- **Class:** **Evidence-limited, absolutely.** Verification is mathematically impossible with current evidence. Any system claiming a high-confidence Phaistos decipherment is, by construction, overclaiming. Confidence ceiling ≈ indeterminate/very low until a second text in the script is excavated.

### 1.6 Proto-Elamite (Iran), c. 3100–2900 BCE
- **Corpus:** ~1,600–1,700 tablets (mostly from Susa), now fully digitised (CDLI). ~1,000+ non-numerical signs catalogued (Dahl's sign list); the texts are overwhelmingly formulaic accounts: heading sign(s), commodity entries, numerical notations, totals `[peer-reviewed]`.
- **State of the art:** The **numerical systems are deciphered** (multiple counting systems — sexagesimal, decimal, bisexagesimal, capacity systems — parallel to proto-cuneiform) `[peer-reviewed]`. A small number of signs have plausible commodity meanings from context and Mesopotamian parallels. The non-numerical "text" remains unread: the language is unidentified (an early form of Elamite is the usual guess, unprovable), and the formulaic accounting format hides grammar — no sentences, almost no syntax.
- **Why unsolved:** No bilingual; unknown language; texts are spreadsheets, not prose — they contain very little of the grammatical signal decipherment feeds on.
- **Class:** **Pattern-limited for the accounting layer (already largely solved there), evidence-limited for language.** A verified *partial* solution (commodity signs, account structure) at high confidence is realistic; a full linguistic decipherment at 97–99% is not, barring new text types or a bilingual.

### 1.7 Cypro-Minoan (Cyprus & Ugarit), c. 1550–1050 BCE
- **Corpus:** ~250–300 inscribed objects, **fewer than 4,000 sign tokens in total**, ~100 sign types; derived from Linear A; ancestor of the (deciphered) Cypriot syllabary `[peer-reviewed]`.
- **State of the art:** Undeciphered. The main recent computational result is structural: unsupervised deep learning on sign shapes (Corazza et al., PLOS ONE, 2022) supports treating Cypro-Minoan as **one unitary script** rather than three sub-scripts (CM1/2/3) `[peer-reviewed]` — progress on the sign inventory, not a reading. The language(s) recorded are unknown (later "Eteocypriot," known from a few inscriptions in the deciphered Cypriot syllabary, is a candidate relative).
- **Why unsolved:** Tiny corpus + unknown language + unresolved sign inventory (scholars still debate which marks are distinct signs). Its parent (Linear A) and its descendant (Cypriot syllabary) bracket it, but Cypro-Minoan's own phonetic values can't simply be inherited with confidence.
- **Class:** **Pattern-limited but corpus-starved** (like Linear A, worse). Structural sub-problems (sign inventory, script unity) are verifiable and partially solved; full semantic decipherment has a low honest ceiling today.

## 2. The solved precedents — what made verification possible

Every accepted decipherment in history was confirmed by the same family of mechanisms. These are the templates any future claim (human or XIIV) will be judged against.

### 2.1 Linear B (Ventris & Chadwick, 1952–53)
- **Assets:** ~6,000 tablets — a corpus big enough for statistics; Alice Kober's distributional analysis (signs sorted into syllabic grid by pure pattern work, without knowing the language); ideograms (pictures of tripods, vessels, livestock) printed next to words; numerals.
- **The verification moment (the gold standard):** In 1953, excavator Carl Blegen applied Ventris's sign values — sight unseen — to **newly excavated, unpublished Pylos tablets**. On tablet PY Ta 641 the values read *ti-ri-po-de* ("tripods," dual) directly beside a tripod ideogram and the numeral 2; a four-handled vessel was labelled *qe-to-ro-we* ("four-eared"), a handle-less one *a-no-we* ("no ears"). The decipherment had **predicted readings on held-out data with pictorial ground truth**. Coincidence was, in Blegen's words, excluded.
- **Lesson:** Prediction on unseen material + independent pictorial anchors = proof. This is the decipherment equivalent of a held-out test set with labels.

### 2.2 Egyptian hieroglyphs (Champollion, 1822)
- **Assets:** The Rosetta Stone — the *same decree* in hieroglyphic, Demotic, and Greek (a true bilingual); royal names isolated in cartouches; and crucially **Coptic**, the living-liturgical descendant of ancient Egyptian, supplying the lexicon and phonology of the underlying language.
- **Verification:** Champollion's sign values, derived from the Ptolemy/Cleopatra cartouches on the bilingual, were applied to cartouches in *other* inscriptions and yielded *Ra-mes-s* (Ramesses) and *Thoth-mes* (Thutmose) — names of native pharaohs known from Greek historians, in texts the system had not been fitted on. The values then read continuous prose across thousands of inscriptions without contradiction.
- **Lesson:** A bilingual anchors the key; a descendant language supplies the dictionary; held-out inscriptions supply the proof.

### 2.3 Ugaritic (Bauer, Dhorme, Virolleaud, 1930)
- **Assets:** A ~30-sign alphabet (small inventory ⇒ frequency analysis is decisive); word dividers preserved (segmentation given for free); close sister languages (Hebrew, Phoenician, Aramaic) with thousands of cognates; thousands of tablets including long mythological poems.
- **Verification:** Three scholars working semi-independently converged on essentially the same values within months; the resulting texts read as coherent West Semitic poetry, ritual, and letters, consistent across the whole corpus. Later-found abecedaries (alphabet-order tablets) confirmed the sign inventory independently.
- **Lesson:** When the language family is known and the corpus is large, decipherment is fast and self-verifying. Ugaritic is what "pattern-limited with all assets present" looks like.

### 2.4 Maya glyphs (Knorozov and successors, 1952–1980s)
- **Assets:** An enormous corpus (thousands of monumental inscriptions + four surviving codices); **living descendant languages** (Yucatec, Ch'ol) still spoken; calendrical/astronomical content that could be checked against astronomy; pictures with glyph captions (image–text pairs = partial known-plaintext); Landa's 16th-century "alphabet," correctly reinterpreted by Knorozov as a syllabary.
- **Verification:** Phonetic readings predicted spellings in new combinations — e.g., a ruler's name written both logographically (a shield, *pakal*) and syllabically (*pa-ka-la*) — and the historical (dynastic) content read from monuments matched the archaeology of the sites (Proskouriakoff's birth/accession patterns). Readings of newly excavated stelae continued to work.
- **Lesson:** Captions beside images are crib material; descendants supply the lexicon; predictions on new monuments supplied decades of held-out confirmation.

### 2.5 Linear Elamite (Desset, 2018–2022) — verification *in progress*
- Claimed decipherment built on royal proper names already known from Akkadian cuneiform sources (a quasi-bilingual anchor: the same kings' names in a known script). Published in a refereed journal and cautiously received — specialists are still testing the values against the full ~40-text corpus `[peer-reviewed, corroboration ongoing]`. Included here deliberately: this is what a decipherment claim looks like *while* the field runs the §2.1–2.4 checks on it.

**The pattern across all five:** no accepted decipherment ever rested on "the fit looks good." Each had at least one of: (i) a bilingual, (ii) a known/descendant language, (iii) pictorial or onomastic anchors, (iv) successful prediction on material not used in fitting. Where none of these exist, no claim has ever been accepted — and (§1.5) sometimes none *can* exist.

## 3. Non-script historical mysteries, classified

| Mystery | Class | Why / ceiling reason |
|---|---|---|
| **Antikythera mechanism** (function & inscriptions) | (a) Pattern-limited — **largely solved** | CT imaging (2005–06) exposed ~3,000+ inscription characters and 37 gears inside the fragments; reconstructions are *verifiable* because working replicas either reproduce the astronomy or don't `[peer-reviewed: Nature 2006; UCL 2021]`. A template case: the "mystery" dissolved when measurement access improved. Residual disputes (e.g., exact calibration date) are narrow. |
| **Zodiac Z340 cipher** (1969) | (a) Pattern-limited — **solved 2020** | Oranchak/Blake/Van Eycke's solution (homophonic substitution + transposition, found by large-scale search with AZdecrypt) was **independently verified by the FBI's CRRU** `[official statement, Dec 2020]`. 340 characters was above the unicity distance; the plaintext is unique and the method reproducible. The model example of a modern verified solution. |
| **Zodiac Z13 & Z32 ciphers** | (b) Evidence-limited (mathematical) | 13 and 32 characters are far below unicity distance; as solver Sam Blake put it, both are "too short to have a unique solution." Any claimed reading is unfalsifiable. Ceiling: no unique solution exists to find. |
| **Kryptos K4** (CIA sculpture, 1990) | (a) Pattern-limited, with a twist | A real plaintext exists; the creator is alive and has released confirmed cribs (BERLIN, CLOCK, NORTHEAST, EAST at set positions), so a proposed solution can be *verified by the author* — a verification channel no ancient case has. But 97 characters leaves little statistical room, and in Sept 2025 the plaintext was reportedly recovered from Sanborn's Smithsonian archives (Kobek & Byrne) and confirmed by Sanborn — while the plaintext stays unpublished and the encryption method remains unreconstructed `[reported 2025; method unsolved]`. K4 currently demonstrates the distinction between *knowing the answer* and *having a derivation*. |
| **Beale ciphers #1 & #3** (1820s) | (b) Evidence-limited | #2 was genuinely solved (key: the Declaration of Independence as book-cipher text). #1 and #3 use unknown key texts; statistical work suggests they may not encode English at all, and a hoax (by the pamphlet's author) cannot be excluded. Without the key document, underdetermined. |
| **Nazca lines survey** (purpose still debated) | (a) Pattern-limited for *detection* | AI-accelerated survey (Sakai et al., PNAS 2024) found **303 new figurative geoglyphs in 6 months**, nearly doubling the known inventory, each field-verified on the ground `[peer-reviewed]`. Detection is checkable; the *purpose* of the lines remains interpretive and probably permanently short of proof-grade certainty. |
| **Shakespeare authorship** | (a) data-rich, **verification-limited** | Full texts survive and stylometry is real evidence, but there is no ground-truth test that could certify an attribution at 97–99%: stylometric results shift with method and corpus choices. Classify as pattern-rich but proof-poor — probability can move, certainty cannot be earned from the surviving record alone. |
| **Jack the Ripper identity** | (b) Evidence-limited | No verified perpetrator DNA or document exists. The 2019 mtDNA claim (Kozminski) was criticised for statistical and provenance flaws and cannot bear the weight placed on it `[contested]`. The surviving record is compatible with several suspects; ceiling far below proof. |
| **Roanoke colony fate** (1590) | (b) Evidence-limited | One carved word ("CROATOAN"), no contemporaneous account of the colonists' end, archaeology suggestive but ambiguous. Multiple end-states fit all evidence. |
| **Amelia Earhart disappearance** (1937) | (b) Evidence-limited | No confirmed wreckage or remains; radio-signal evidence is fragmentary and contested; three incompatible hypotheses (crashed-and-sank, Gardner Island, Marshall Islands) each fit subsets of the record. Resolution requires new physical evidence, not better pattern-matching on old evidence. |
| **Wow! signal** (1977) | (b) Evidence-limited | A single 72-second observation, never repeated despite extensive follow-up. There is no dataset to analyse further; candidate natural explanations (e.g., comet hydrogen clouds, 2017) remain disputed `[contested]`. |
| **Lost tombs** (Alexander the Great, Cleopatra, Genghis Khan) | (b) Evidence-limited (search problems) | The textual record is exhausted and contradictory; these are *location searches* over large physical areas. AI can rank search areas but cannot convert absence of evidence into 97–99% confidence — only excavation can. |

**Rule of thumb the table demonstrates:** if a mystery's resolution can be *checked* (replica runs, FBI re-derivation, held-out tablets, ground-truthed geoglyphs), it is pattern-limited and fair game. If every surviving fact is already public and several incompatible stories fit them all, the mystery is evidence-limited and the honest deliverable is the ceiling itself.

## 4. AI & computational efforts — measured results only

### 4.1 Ithaca (DeepMind + universities, *Nature* 2022) — restoration, not decipherment
Transformer trained on ~63,000 ancient Greek inscriptions; tested on 7,811 held-out inscriptions `[peer-reviewed]`:
- Restoring damaged text: **62% accuracy alone**; historians alone 25%; **historians + Ithaca 72%**.
- Geographic attribution: **71%** accuracy. Dating: within **±30 years**.
- Crucial scope note: Greek is a *known* language with a massive digitised corpus. Ithaca measures what ML does with every asset present — it is an upper-bound reference, not evidence about undeciphered scripts.

### 4.2 Neural decipherment (MIT CSAIL: Luo, Cao & Barzilay)
- **ACL 2019, "Neural Decipherment via Minimum-Cost Flow"** `[peer-reviewed]`: seq2seq character alignment + min-cost-flow cognate matching, using documented sound-change patterns as priors. On **Ugaritic**: +5.5% absolute over the previous state of the art (Snyder et al.'s 2010 Bayesian model). On **Linear B**: **67.3% of cognates correctly translated** to Greek equivalents — the first automatic Linear B results, obtained in hours.
- **2021 follow-up** (phonetic-prior generative model) `[peer-reviewed]`: handles *unsegmented* scripts and unknown closest language; validated on Gothic and Ugaritic; applied to undeciphered **Iberian**, producing proposed readings that are — by the framework of this document — unverifiable, and presented by the authors as such.
- **The load-bearing condition:** both systems require a **known related language with a cognate lexicon**. That asset exists for Ugaritic (Hebrew) and Linear B (Greek). It does not exist for Indus, Linear A (as a language), Voynich, or Cypro-Minoan — which is precisely why those remain open. ML has not removed the bilingual/descendant requirement; it has industrialised the work *after* that requirement is met.

### 4.3 Hauer & Kondrak on Voynich (2016–2018) — the canonical "AI solved it" overclaim
- Method: statistical language identification (character n-gram models) over the Universal Declaration of Human Rights in 380 languages; Hebrew scored highest. A constraint solver then anagrammed Voynich words, dropped vowels, and matched ~80% of words in one passage against a Hebrew dictionary `[peer-reviewed paper; claim rejected by field]`.
- Reception: medievalists and computational linguists (Davis, Argamon, Koppel) rejected the conclusion: the method returns a "best" language even for gibberish (the runner-up matches included Malay), it used modern rather than medieval Hebrew, Hebrew's reading direction and one-letter-word behaviour clash with the manuscript, and anagramming + free vowel insertion has enough combinatorial freedom to "find" words in noise — the authors themselves conceded the results "could be interpreted either as tantalizing clues… or simply as artifacts of the combinatorial power of anagramming and language models."
- Press coverage nonetheless ran "AI decodes Voynich manuscript" headlines worldwide. **Status: debunked as a decipherment; retained as a cautionary benchmark in how not to report confidence.**

### 4.4 Cheshire's "proto-Romance" (2019) — retracted within days
- Gerard Cheshire published a Voynich "solution" (the language is "proto-Romance") in *Romance Studies* with a University of Bristol press release claiming success "where countless cryptographers… have failed." Specialists dismantled the method (arbitrary letter substitutions, subjective translation choices); **Bristol removed and retracted the press release**, disavowing affiliation `[documented retraction]`. Lesson for any benchmark program: venue publication ≠ verification; only held-out prediction is.

### 4.5 Computational work on Indus
- Rao et al., *Science* 2009: conditional entropy of Indus sign sequences falls within the range of linguistic systems `[peer-reviewed]`. Markov-model work (PNAS 2009) extracted syntactic structure. Farmer/Sproat/Witzel's rebuttal argued the non-linguistic comparison sets were invented and the measure cannot distinguish language from non-language at these text lengths `[peer-reviewed exchange; unresolved]`. Net measured outcome after 15+ years of ML/statistical work: **zero accepted sign readings**. The bottleneck is not compute.
- Institutional benchmark: **Tamil Nadu US$1M prize (Jan 2025)** for a decipherment the scholarly community accepts — still unclaimed. This is effectively a standing, externally-judged benchmark with the §2 verification bar built in.

### 4.6 Computational work on Aegean/Cypriot scripts
- The 2024 *Computational Linguistics* systematic review of computational approaches to Linear A, Phaistos, Cypro-Minoan and neighbours concludes: real progress on sign inventories, numeral/fraction systems, and corpus infrastructure (e.g., the SigLA database); **no decipherment** `[peer-reviewed]`.
- Corazza et al. 2022 (Cypro-Minoan, §1.7) and the Linear A fraction study (2020) are the strongest computational results in the area — all *structural or numerical*, none semantic.

### 4.7 AI pattern-finding beyond text
- **Nazca (PNAS 2024):** AI survey + field verification nearly doubled the known geoglyph inventory in 6 months (§3). The verification was physical: every candidate was ground-checked. AI proposed, evidence disposed.

## 5. Synthesis for the XIIV target list

1. **The verification bar is historical, not optional.** Every accepted solution in §2 passed a held-out/prediction test with independent anchors. XIIV's 97–99% axis is credible exactly insofar as a target admits that test. Targets that do (Voynich — large corpus, held-out pages; Rongorongo — multi-tablet cross-validation against a known language; Linear A/Cypro-Minoan — structural and numerical layers) belong on the target list with the test specified *in advance*.
2. **The strongest near-term "wins" are sub-decipherment layers with hard checks:** sign inventories, numeral systems, scribal-hand attribution, generation-process identification (for Voynich: cipher vs language vs generated pseudo-text is itself a decidable, evidence-testable question), and restoration of *known*-language texts (the Ithaca regime, where 62–72% measured accuracy is the honest scale of current capability).
3. **Full semantic decipherments at 97–99% verified confidence are not honestly available today for Indus, Phaistos, or the language layer of Proto-Elamite/Linear A/Cypro-Minoan** — not because the solver is insufficiently powerful, but because the surviving evidence cannot certify any solution. On these targets, the correct omega-level output is a *proved ceiling*: a demonstration that the evidence underdetermines the answer, quantified (unicity analysis, count of incompatible keys fitting the corpus). That is itself a result no current system publishes — and unlike a fake "decipherment," it can be verified.
4. **What would change the classification:** new evidence, not new compute — a bilingual inscription (Indus/Linear A), a second Phaistos-script text, longer Indus texts, or a proper-name anchor in a known record (the Linear Elamite route). A benchmark program should therefore pair the solver with an evidence watch: the targets' classes are properties of the archaeological record and can move.
